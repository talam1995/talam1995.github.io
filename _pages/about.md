I am a PhD student in Computer Science at New Mexico State University and a Graduate Research Assistant in the NMSU Research Cores Program. Coming from a background in statistics and data science, I am interested in bringing statistical and machine learning methods together to understand complex populations and to support data-driven decision-making.

## Research

My dissertation research explores how real-world survey data can be used to build reliable, population-level representations of people for studying policy questions. Broadly, the work involves:

- **Survey calibration:** adjusting survey samples so they better reflect the population they represent.
- **Synthetic populations:** generating privacy-preserving synthetic data that retains key statistical relationships.
- **Causal modeling:** using structural causal models to explore counterfactual "what-if" scenarios.
- **Validation and uncertainty:** assessing how errors at each stage affect the reliability of the results.

## Background

I trained as a statistician. After a B.S. in Statistics from the University of Dhaka, I spent three and a half years as an Actuarial Associate at the Syngenta Foundation for Sustainable Agriculture in Bangladesh, developing weather index insurance products for farmers. I then earned an M.S. in Statistics and Data Science at The University of Texas at El Paso, where I received the **Academic and Research Excellence Award** and my NSF-supported [thesis](https://scholarworks.utep.edu/open_etd/4219/) was nominated for the College of Science best thesis award. Before joining NMSU, I worked as a research associate at UTEP on projects in public health informatics, groundwater risk, and mobility in aging populations.
