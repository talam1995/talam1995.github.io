I am a PhD student in Computer Science at New Mexico State University. My research focuses on developing high-fidelity Human/Social Digital Twins through the integration of synthetic population generation, statistical data synthesis, survey calibration, structural causal modeling, and computational simulation.

My research aims to create realistic computational representations of human populations that can support evidence-based analysis, policy evaluation, and intervention simulation. I am particularly interested in combining statistical methods, causal inference, and agent-based modeling to improve the reliability and validity of digital representations of societies.

## Research Interests

- Human and Social Digital Twins
- Synthetic Population Generation
- Synthetic Microdata and Statistical Data Synthesis
- Survey Weighting and Calibration Methods
- Structural Causal Models and Causal Inference
- Agent-Based Modeling
- LLM-Based Behavioral Agents
- Statistical Validation of Synthetic Populations

## Current Research

My doctoral research investigates methodologies for constructing high-fidelity synthetic populations from survey and population-level data. The goal is to develop validated computational populations that preserve important statistical relationships and enable causal intervention analysis within digital twin environments.
