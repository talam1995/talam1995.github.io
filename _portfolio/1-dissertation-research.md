*Dissertation research, New Mexico State University, 2025 – present*

Surveys are one of our richest sources of information about people, but they are samples, often imperfect ones, and they cannot tell us directly how a population would respond to a change in policy. My dissertation research looks at how survey data can be turned into trustworthy population-level representations that can be used to explore such questions.

The work brings together statistical methods for correcting survey data, privacy-preserving synthetic data generation, and causal modeling for "what-if" analysis. A central focus is validation: understanding how much we can trust the results, and where uncertainty enters along the way.
